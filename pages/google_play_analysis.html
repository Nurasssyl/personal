Google Play Store Data Analysis

The Google Play Store serves as a digital marketplace offering a variety of apps. By analyzing its data, this project uncovers trends in app categories, user ratings, downloads, and pricing to better understand the dynamics of app popularity and user preferences.

Category Analysis

1. Distribution of Apps by Category

Categories such as "Family" and "Games" dominate in the number of apps available. These categories attract developers due to their broad audience appeal, making them some of the most competitive spaces in the marketplace.

2. Number of Installs by Category

The "Games" and "Social" categories stand out with the highest number of downloads. This indicates the universal demand for entertainment and communication platforms among users.

3. Average Rating by Category

"Education" and "Events" categories have the highest average ratings, reflecting user satisfaction with apps in these domains. This suggests the importance of quality content in niche categories.

4. Number of Apps by Year and Category

The number of apps peaked in 2017, especially in the "Family" and "Games" categories. This growth aligns with the increasing smartphone adoption during that period.

Ratings Distribution

5. Overall Ratings Distribution

Most apps achieve ratings above 4.0, indicating that the majority of users are satisfied with the available apps. This trend emphasizes the competitive standards of quality in the store.

6. Ratings by Category and App Type

Paid apps generally receive higher ratings across categories, likely due to users' higher expectations and the targeted nature of such apps.

Factors Influencing Popularity

7. Relationship Between Reviews and Ratings

Apps with more reviews tend to maintain a stable rating, showing that user feedback contributes to both credibility and visibility in the store.

8. Effect of App Size on Ratings

In categories like "Games," larger app sizes correlate with higher ratings, possibly due to the inclusion of more features or better graphics.

9. Correlation Between Numeric Variables

The heatmap highlights that the number of installs is strongly correlated with the number of reviews, demonstrating the role of user engagement in app popularity.

Free vs Paid Apps

10. Proportion of Free and Paid Apps

Free apps dominate the store, but paid apps often secure slightly higher ratings. This reflects the higher expectations and quality associated with paid apps.

11. Average Price of Paid Apps by Category

"Medical" and "Books" categories include the most expensive apps, catering to specific and professional needs.

Detailed Ratings Analysis

12. Average Rating and Number of Apps

Categories with a higher number of apps, such as "Games," tend to have slightly lower average ratings due to increased competition and varying quality.

13. Ratings Distribution of Free and Paid Apps

Free apps often have a wider range of ratings, whereas paid apps cluster around higher ratings, reflecting the expectations of paying users.

14. Relationship Between Rating, Reviews, and Price

Higher-priced apps with strong ratings attract more reviews, indicating their ability to engage a loyal user base despite higher costs.

Conclusion

The analysis reveals critical insights into app performance on the Google Play Store:

  • "Games" and "Family" categories dominate in terms of app count and downloads.
  • Paid apps generally achieve higher ratings due to targeted audiences and enhanced quality.
  • User engagement, as reflected by reviews and installs, is key to an app's success.
  • The peak in app additions during 2017 aligns with the growth of the mobile app ecosystem.
