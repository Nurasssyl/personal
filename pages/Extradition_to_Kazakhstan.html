About the Map

This map shows the current landscape of extradition treaties between Kazakhstan and other countries as of 2025. It reflects official bilateral agreements, ongoing negotiations, and real-world extraditions that occurred even in the absence of formal treaties.

Kazakhstan has actively expanded its legal cooperation network over the past decade, especially with European, Asian, and Latin American nations. Today, the country has over 25 formal extradition treaties, and negotiations are underway with more than a dozen others.

  • 🟦 Countries in blue have ratified bilateral extradition treaties.
  • 🟨 Countries in yellow are engaged in negotiations.
  • 🟧 Countries in orange have extradited individuals without formal treaties.
  • ⬜ Countries in gray have no current mechanism in place.

This map will be updated as new treaties are signed and cooperation expands.

Facts and Notable Cases

🔵 Countries with Bilateral Treaties

Kazakhstan has signed and ratified extradition treaties with over 25 countries. These include strategic partners across Europe, Asia, and Latin America. Here are some key events:

  • Spain: Treaty signed in 2012; enabled streamlined legal cooperation with the EU.
  • Italy: Treaty ratified in 2015, following increased financial investigations.
  • Brazil: A milestone treaty signed in 2018, boosting ties with Latin America.
  • UAE: Extradition treaty signed in 2009 led to several high-profile returns, including officials accused of embezzlement.
  • Cyprus: Ratified in 2023, with a focus on financial crimes and offshore fraud.

🟨 Countries in Negotiation

Kazakhstan is actively negotiating extradition or legal cooperation treaties with numerous states:

  • Germany: Despite no treaty, discussions are ongoing due to repeated requests involving financial fugitives.
  • France: Although legal assistance exists, Kazakhstan seeks a full extradition agreement.
  • Israel: Talks have included prisoner transfer mechanisms and mutual legal aid.
  • Argentina, Chile, Colombia, Guatemala, El Salvador, Nicaragua, Uruguay: Engagements reflect Kazakhstan's expanding outreach to Latin America.
  • Slovakia and Portugal: Negotiations part of Kazakhstan–EU judicial alignment.

🔶 Extraditions Without Treaty

Kazakhstan has successfully secured the return of several individuals from countries without formal extradition agreements:

  • USA: In 2017, fraud suspect Talgat Nauryzbayev was deported by U.S. authorities and taken into custody in Kazakhstan.
  • France: In 2023, Zhanar Ablyazov, associate of Mukhtar Ablyazov, was extradited — a rare case given previous refusals.
  • Montenegro: In 2021, a former accountant of betting company "Olimp" was extradited.
  • Cameroon: In 2024, a Kazakh national on Interpol notice was returned from Yaoundé.
  • Sweden and Poland: Individuals were extradited on the basis of reciprocity and court approval.
  • Northern Cyprus: Cooperation resulted in extradition despite political complexities.

These cases show that even in the absence of treaties, Kazakhstan's law enforcement actively works through diplomatic and legal channels to secure accountability.
